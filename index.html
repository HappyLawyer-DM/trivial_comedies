Eine triviale Komödie für ernsthafte Leute — with apologies to Oscar Wilde.

Eight small browser games about the legal profession: one arbitration told three times over — from counsel's lectern, from the expert's chair, and from the war room three floors below — plus a law firm from partnership to first-year dungeon to the secretariat that actually runs it, the fourteenth floor at quarter's end — and one full campaign: seven years from name badges to the partnership vote. Pick a class, watch the dice, keep your coffee bar filled. No account, no install, no mercy from the printer.

Colophon

These games were built in long conversation with Claude, Anthropic's AI assistant — co-author, tireless playtester, and patient re-collator of bundles. Which gives the library a second, quieter joke: it is also a portrait of what an AI, raised on the profession's collective writing, imagines the law to feel like — the hearing weeks and the partners' retreats, the billable targets and the pink message slips, the quarter's end on the fourteenth floor; the war stories, the understatement, the reverence for the record, the pastries. A portrait, though, is not a photograph: the model learned the profession from what the internet chose to write down, and the internet records its grievances far more diligently than its ordinary Tuesdays. Some hardships in these games are therefore exaggerated, others are satire outright, and the all-nighters arrive at a frequency no real firm would recognise, let alone accept. None of it should be confused with reality — least of all with any actual firm, colleague, case or client. If practitioners recognise themselves in these rooms anyway, the credit belongs to everyone who ever wrote the war stories down. The judgment calls, the professional realism and the final say remained, throughout, human.
